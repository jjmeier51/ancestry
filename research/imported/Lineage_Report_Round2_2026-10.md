# Johnny Meier Lineage: Round 2 Deep Dive (October 2026)

This report builds on the first report, `Johnny_Meier_Lineage_Report_2026-10.md`, and doesn't repeat it. Each finding is labelled:

- **Confirmed**: a record I read says so directly, or two independent records agree.
- **Probable**: strong evidence that points one way, but one document is still missing.
- **Speculative**: worth chasing, but not established.

Full sources, with links, are in `research/notes/round2/` (one file per family line). Living people were not researched beyond the names already in the tree.

---

## 1. The short version

- **The flyer in the family.** Maj. Leo G. Heffernan, a brother of great-great-grandmother Catherine Heffernan Pringle, set a **250-mph speed record on 24 Mar 1923**, which *Time* covered. He later ran the Army Air Corps Technical School and commanded France Field in Panama. His obituary says **Jimmy Doolittle and Claire Chennault served under him**.
- **A mayor, a senator and a Titanic victim are among your cousins.** Through Bull Smith and the Long Island lines, you are cousin to:
  - New York City Mayor Cornelius Van Wyck Lawrence;
  - U.S. Senator and General John Smith;
  - Sir Willoughby Jones, a British MP;
  - Rear Admiral Daniel L. Braine;
  - James Clinch Smith, who died on the Titanic;
  - Bessie Smith White, wife of the architect Stanford White.
- **The Irish line now reaches County Tipperary.** The Heffernans came from the parish of **Killenaule and Gortnahoe** in Co. Tipperary. They were coal miners there before they mined anthracite in Pennsylvania. Two great-uncles were **transported to Australia** after a constable was killed in 1834.
- **Your grandmother Mollie is identified.** "Mollie" Petriello was **Carmela Gianetta** (1910–2000) of Dunmore. Her parents came from **Bisaccia**, a second Irpinian hometown alongside Guardia Lombardi.
- **Helen Cognetti's maiden name is almost certainly Ferlaino.** Census clerks wrote it as "Farino."
- **The Long Island gap is now probably closed.** Temperance Smith's parents (James Smith and Jerusha Topping) and John Tuthill's father are now probable, from a 1968 Tuttle genealogy.
- **Your grandfather James C. Meier died in Levittown, Bucks County, PA, on 13 Aug 2008** (probable). This is the first record of the family outside Luzerne County.

## 4. Stories and interesting facts

### Colonial Long Island and the Hudson Valley

- **Bull Smith's reluctant wedding.** Richard "Bull" Smith tried to back out of marrying Sarah Hammond in 1640. The marriage went ahead anyway and founded the whole Smithtown line.
- **"Old Crib."** Sarah Hammond Smith's blunderbuss, "Old Crib", was passed down in the family as an heirloom.
- **A colt, on conditions.** William Hammond's will left his grandson Adam a colt "if he behave obediently."
- **The Phantom Ship.** Francis Brewster was lost aboard New Haven's "Great Shippe" in 1646. A ghostly image of that ship was later said to appear over the harbor.
- **"Wast paper."** Francis Brewster's widow Lucy was tried in 1646 for calling the minister's sermon "wast paper."
- **Topping's Purchase.** Land on eastern Long Island was bought from the Native owners with wampum.
- **Saved from the navy.** In 1706 Governor Cornbury ordered André Lamoureux released after he had been impressed into naval service.
- **A pointed will.** Thomas Lamoreaux's 1829 will disinherited his son James and left a share to his disabled grandson Jewel. It also left his wife Keturah his best horse.
- **A family regiment.** In 1776 two of Keturah's Smith uncles were militia captains, and her future husband's family served as officers under them.

### Ireland to Pennsylvania (the Heffernans)

- **Miners twice over.** The Heffernans dug coal in the Slieveardagh hills of Tipperary, one of Ireland's few coalfields, before they dug anthracite in Luzerne County.
- **Transported to Australia.** Great-uncles James and Patrick Heffernan were sent to New South Wales after Constable Feeney was killed at Ballynonty in 1834.
- **A shooting at Leadville.** Anna Heffernan Dillon's husband was shot in the Colorado silver boomtown of Leadville in 1880.
- **The Sunday paper.** Thomas F. Heffernan's *Sunday Independent* is digitized for 1913–58 on POWER Library. It is a good place to look for family notices.

### Italy (Irpinia and Calabria)

- **Guardia Lombardi** was founded by the Lombards around 571–591 as a lookout fort. A plague in 1656 killed 1,110 of its 1,475 people. The 1980 Irpinia earthquake destroyed more than half its buildings.
- **St. Rocco's in Dunmore.** About 40 families from Guardia bought a former Presbyterian church there and said the first Mass in October 1905. John Petriello's grocery, already open by 1897, would have served that early colony.
- **Bisaccia**, Mollie's family's town, sent about 916 people to America between 1830 and 1912, with the peak in 1906, the year Frank Giannetta came. Mario Procaccino, the 1969 Democratic nominee for mayor of New York City, was born there; no kinship is known.
- **San Mango d'Aquino** was founded in the early 1600s by a branch of the d'Aquino family, the family of St. Thomas Aquinas, and its church is dedicated to him. The Ferlaino house at 1306 Diamond Ave took in newly arrived San Mango men as boarders.

### Black Forest

- **The Jägerhof.** In 1751 Josef Heitzler, gamekeeper to the Counts of Kageneck, bought the farm later called the Jägerhof at auction. Its sawmill still operates. Josef Heitzler "vom Jägerhof" was mayor of Unteribental from 1919 to 1924.
- **The youngest son inherits.** Black Forest farms passed whole to one son, usually the youngest. The other children became weavers, sawyers, gamekeepers or emigrants.
- **A one-way ticket.** In 1861 Kirchzarten paid half the fare to send Xaver Ruh, a repeat thief, to America rather than see him steal a fourth time.
- **Kirchzarten was part of Austria.** The city of Freiburg owned Kirchzarten from 1492 to 1805, within Habsburg "Further Austria", before it passed to Baden.

### Pennsylvania in the 1900s

- **The Lattimer Massacre** of 1897 took place just outside Hazleton while Freiderich and Maria Meier lived there.
- **The Agnes flood** of 1972 washed graves out of the Forty Fort cemetery.
- **Levittown, PA** was built between 1952 and 1958. In 1957 it drew national headlines when the Myers family, its first Black residents, faced mob harassment.

## 5. Regional ties, updated

- **Long Island.** The ties are still colonial only, but stronger: Smithtown, the Tuthills, Topping's Purchase and the Lawrence cousins. Two speculative Henry Meier deaths, at Huntington (1931) and Hempstead (1937), are the only possible modern link.
- **Northeastern PA.** Every American line converges here:
  - the Meiers in Hazleton and then Wilkes-Barre;
  - the Pringles and Heffernans in Plymouth and Wilkes-Barre;
  - the Petriellos, Gianettas, Cognettis and Ferlainos in Scranton and Dunmore.

  James C. Meier's death in Levittown, Bucks County, is the first record of the family outside the coal region.
- **Virginia.** Nothing new. First Lady Julia Gardiner Tyler is still the only cousin tie.
