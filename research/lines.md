#### Meier line (Mayer of Munzingen)

**Path from John:** John → Thomas F. Meier → James C. Meier → William F. Meier → Henry J. Meier → Friedrich Mayer ("Freiderich Meier") → Heinrich ("Henericus") Mayer → Michael Mayer.

**Summary.** This is John's direct paternal surname line. It is now proven generation by generation: US census pages and marriage applications cover the American links, and the original Munzingen civil-register duplicates (Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg, Staatsarchiv Freiburg, L 10 Nr. 1590 for 1810–39 and L 10 Nr. 1591 for 1840–70) cover the German ones. The family came from **Munzingen**, a Tuniberg wine village about 10 km south-west of Freiburg im Breisgau in Baden, today part of the city of Freiburg. It was **not** Kirchzarten. The Ancestry tree and the earlier reports had assumed Kirchzarten because they thought the wife's family came from there; neither side has any record tie to Kirchzarten.

**Origins.** Michael Mayer was a citizen (Bürger) and carpenter (Zimmermann) of Munzingen; his wife and dates are unknown. His son Heinrich (b. c.1796), a mason and later a master mason, married the orphan Juliana Senn (b. c.1798, the illegitimate daughter of Juliana Senn) on 16 Oct 1820 and was "married with house No. 68". They had at least ten children, 1821–1840. Friedrich (b. 2 Mar 1835), also a mason and a Munzingen citizen, married Maria Anna Birkenmaier on 20 Oct 1862. Four generations worked in the building trades: Michael was a carpenter, Heinrich a mason, Friedrich a stone mason, and Henry an iron moulder.

**Emigration.** Friedrich, Maria and four small children (Friedrich b. 1862, Franziska b. 1863, Maria b. 1865 and Heinrich/Henry b. 1866) emigrated between Nov 1866 and Sept 1869. The most likely year is **1868**, which Henry himself reported in the 1910 census; the other children's census entries give 1867 or 1868, and Clarissa was born in Pennsylvania about Sept 1869. The ship and port have not been found. The Bremen Deutsche Auswanderer-Datenbank has no matching family group for 1866–69, so Le Havre or Antwerp is likely. No emigration note was found in the margins of the 1835 or 1862 entries. By June 1870 they were in Hazleton's East Ward, Luzerne Co., PA, where Friedrich worked as a stone mason; his neighbour Anthony Werner was another Baden stone mason. Henry moved through Hazleton (1880–94), Wilkes-Barre Ward 16 (1897–1900), Tamaqua, Schuylkill Co. (c.1903–06) and then Wilkes-Barre (14 Mill St 1910, 298 Barney St 1920, 16 Barney St 1926–40). William was a coal-company clerk and supervisor in Wilkes-Barre (55 Cedar St, 1930–50). James died in Levittown, Bucks Co., PA, in 2008, the first record of the family outside the coal region.

**Spelling history: Mayer → Meier.** There was no formal name change; the spelling drifted with whoever wrote it down.
- The Munzingen registers write **Mayer** throughout, 1820–1862: the 1820 marriage of "Henericus Mayer", the 1821 and 1835 baptisms, and the 1862 marriage of "Friedrich Mayer". Latin and FamilySearch index forms include "Henricus Mayer".
- The 1870 US census has "**Meyer**, Frederick".
- The 1880 census has "**Myer**, Friedrich" ("Fredrick Myer").
- The 1892 Luzerne marriage docket No. 11054 for Henry J. Meier is the **first "Meier"**. A clerk wrote it, so Henry may not have chosen the spelling.
- The PA death indexes have "Meier" for Mary A. (1911) and Frederick (1913).
- From 1910 on the spelling is consistently **Meier**: the 1910 and 1920 censuses, the 1926 marriage application, the 1930 and 1950 censuses, and the 1963 death index. The one exception is "**Meyer**, Henry J." in the 1940 census.
- The Ancestry tree shows "Henry J MEIER MY…" and "Henery Meier", and "Henery" comes from the Latin-style "Henericus" in the 1820 entry.
- The Birkenmayer name varies in the same way: Birkenmayer (1836, 1837, 1842), Birkenmaier (1853, 1862), "Bergenmyer" (1880 census), and "Berkmeier" (Find a Grave index).

#### Birkenmayer line (Birkenmaier / Birkenmeier)

**Path from John:** John → Thomas → James C. → William F. → Henry J. → **Maria Anna Birkenmayer (1837)** → **Servatius Birkenmayer (1807)** → **Johann Evangelist Birkenmayer (1771)** → (tree) Matthias Birkenmayer (1739–1803) & Caecilia Hohler (1752) → (tree) Stephan Birkenmaier (1694–1768).

**Origins.** The family was established in **Munzingen**, not the Dreisamtal. Records there include:
- Johann Ev. Birkenmayer (10 Jan 1771 – 17 Dec 1844), a widower and day labourer. In 1836 his title reads "herrschaftl. Waibel[?]", perhaps a seigneurial bailiff or beadle.
- His wife Magdalena Erb, who had died by 1836 (the tree gives 1771–1829).
- Their son Servatius (b. 4 May 1807, or 1806 by his death age; d. 19 Apr 1853), a master glazier and joiner/cabinetmaker and a citizen of Munzingen. He married (Maria) Agatha Heizler on 17 Oct 1836 (the FS index says 27 Oct).
- Seven children: Maria Anna (29 Mar 1837), Rosa (1839, died 1839), Joseph (15 Mar 1842), Rosa (1844–45), Karolina (1846–69), Gabriel E. (20 Jan 1849) and Heinrich (1852–53).

Other Munzingen Birkenmaiers whose relationship to this family is not worked out:
- Johannes Birkenmayer, father of Maria Anna (1810–1865), who married Salesius Schuhmacher.
- Johann Birkenmeier (b. 1808), illegitimate son of Walburga Birkenberger, a master mason in Basel. His daughter Agatha Carolina died in Basel in 1844.
- Infant deaths in 1832.
- The mason Anton Birkenmaier, whose wife was née Scherer.
- A Joseph Birkenmaier who was a marriage witness in 1810.

**Emigration.**
- Maria Anna went to Hazleton with her husband in about 1868.
- Her brother Gabriel lived with them in 1880 (as "Gabriel Bergenmyer", 29, gardener) and later went to Sandusky, Ohio, where he died 2 Dec 1896.
- Her brother Joseph also went to Sandusky and died there 3 Nov 1904 (identity probable, from the Ancestry index).
- Munzingen had an emigrant pipeline to northern Ohio: the village chronicle lists Franz Michel, who left in 1854 and is "assumed to be in Ohio", and Rosalia Dämpfle (b. 1829 Munzingen) died in 1904 at Sherman, Huron Co., OH.

**How the line was found.** Round 1 found no Servatius anywhere. Round 2 established that the Kirchzarten parish database (about 30,000 people) has no Servatius, no Johann E., no Erb and no Hohler. It then found an Ancestry index snippet naming "Servatius Birkenmayer & Agatha Heitzler" as parents of Joseph Birkenmeier, b. 15 Mar 1842 "Münzingen", d. Sandusky 1904. Round 3 read the Munzingen originals and confirmed the line.

#### Heizler / Heitzler line (Kageneck gamekeepers)

**Path from John:** John → … → Maria Anna Birkenmayer → **Agatha Heizler (b. 26 Jun 1813 Munzingen)** → **Joseph Heizler**, herrschaftlicher Jäger (d. 1815–35) → (probable) **Jakob/Jacob Heitzler** (c.1740–1806, Kageneck gamekeeper in Unteribental) & Catharina Begelspacher → (tree) Martinus [= Mathias] Heitzler (1699–1728) & Othilia Schirk → (tree) Andreas Haizler (1665).

**Origins.** The Heitzlers were gamekeepers and forest wardens for the **Counts of Kageneck**.
- The Kagenecks held Munzingen from the mid-17th century and built its Schloss in 1672.
- They acquired **Stegen in 1702**, with the lordship over the Ibental, a side valley east of Freiburg in the Dreisamtal.
- The Dreisamtal database calls Jakob Heitzler "Kageneck'scher Jäger in Unteribental, Waldhüter" and records his part in a 1789 lawsuit. His wife Katharina Begelspacher's father was also a Kageneck gamekeeper.
- Agatha's father **Joseph Heizler** was the "herrschaftlicher Jäger" (seigneurial gamekeeper) at Munzingen. He married M. Anna Federer of Munzingen there on 26 Dec 1799 (FS tree event).
- Joseph and M. Anna's recorded children are Friedrich (8 Mar 1809), Agatha (26 Jun 1813) and Veronica (d. 29 Apr 1815, of measles). Joseph was alive in 1815 and dead by Oct 1835.
- That a Kageneck gamekeeper's son should serve as the Kagenecks' gamekeeper at their Munzingen seat is how an Ibental gamekeeping family would end up in Munzingen.

**The history of the question:**
- **The tree** had "Maria A Heitzler 1813–1867", daughter of Joseph Heitzler 1773–1825 and Maria Anna Federer 1772–1844, and Joseph as the son of Jacob(us) Heitzler (d. 1806) and Catharina Begelspacher (d. 1796).
- **The deep-dive and round 2** found no Joseph among Jakob's recorded children (Andreas 1764, Agathe 1771, Franziska 1779, Jakob 1781) and proposed three rival candidates for the 1813 woman:
  - **A.** Maria Anna Heitzler, b. 8 Apr 1813 Unteribental, daughter of the Ibental schoolteacher Joseph Heitzler (1765–1828) and Maria Pfister. She married J. G. Gremmelspacher in 1855.
  - **C.** The Wehrlehof family of Joseph Heitzler × Maria Rombach, in Oberried-Vörlinsbach.
  - **D.** "Agatha Heitzler", from the Ancestry snippet.
- **Round 3 settled it:** the wife was (Maria) **Agatha** Heizler, daughter of Joseph Heizler & M. Anna Federer of Munzingen. Candidates A and C are **ruled out**. The Stegen "Agatha Heitzler c.1810" (daughter of Andreas Heitzler & Agatha Schlegel) is a different woman, probably a first cousin.
- **Joseph's parentage has been upgraded to probable.** Jakob's recorded children leave gaps (1765–70 and 1772–78) where a Joseph b. c.1773 fits, and Agatha's name repeats Jakob's daughter Agathe (b. 1771).
